Controls & Navigation

Navigate the complex plane in first-person or free-flight mode, or trigger automated walks along analytic structures.

Action Shortcut Behavior
Movement W A S D Translate forward, left, backward, and right across the complex plane.
Elevation Space Ascend above the terrain (double-press to reset camera height).
Sprint Shift Hold to increase movement speed across large regions.
Slow Walk Ctrl Hold for fine-grained movement near zeros, poles, or branch cuts.
Settings Menu Esc Toggle the function, environment, and rendering configuration menu.
Critical Walking Ctrl+C When viewing \(\zeta(s)\), lock onto and walk along the critical line \(\operatorname{Re}(s) = \tfrac{1}{2}\).
Newton Walking Ctrl+Z Automatically follow Newton steps \(-f(s)/f'(s)\) to converge to a nearby zero of the active function.
Section · 02

Mathematical Functions

The explorer supports standard complex families—including trigonometric, exponential, rational, and logarithmic functions—alongside special functions of analytic number theory. The centerpiece of the project is the Riemann zeta function \( \zeta(s) \).

Function · 02.1

The Riemann zeta function

Defined by the Dirichlet series \( \zeta(s) = \sum_{n=1}^\infty n^{-s} \) for \( \operatorname{Re}(s) > 1 \), the function is analytically continued to the rest of the complex plane. Our shader and C++ implementation uses the alternating Dirichlet eta representation for numerical stability when \( \operatorname{Re}(s) > 0.5 \):

\[ \zeta(s) = \frac{1}{1 - 2^{1-s}} \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{n^s} \]

For \( \operatorname{Re}(s) \le 0.5 \), the explorer uses Riemann's reflection formula to continue the function across the critical strip and into the left half-plane:

\[ \zeta(s) = 2^s \pi^{s-1} \sin\!\left(\frac{\pi s}{2}\right) \Gamma(1-s)\,\zeta(1-s) \]

In this regime, the reflected point \( 1-s \) satisfies \( \operatorname{Re}(1-s) \ge 0.5 \), so \( \zeta(1-s) \) can be evaluated directly via the Dirichlet eta representation.

Note on GPU Precision

Real-time terrain calculations are performed in GPU shaders using float32 arithmetic. This introduces numerical limitations and potential artifacts as the magnitude of the imaginary part \( |t| \) increases, due to rapid oscillation and large intermediate values. These effects are especially pronounced in the \( \sin \) and \( \Gamma \) factors of the reflection formula, both of which grow exponentially in magnitude.

Borwein's Algorithm

To compute the Riemann zeta function accurately and rapidly, we also implement Borwein's algorithm. This Chebyshev-accelerated alternating series provides exponential convergence across the critical strip. We specifically use it in our C++ zero finder (`float64`) to refine candidate positions and lock onto non-trivial zeros on the critical line with high precision.

Function · 02.2

The Dirichlet beta function

The explorer implements the Dirichlet beta function \( \beta(s) \)—the \(L\)-function associated with the non-principal character modulo 4—using its alternating series representation together with Euler's reflection formula:

\[ \beta(s) = \sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{(-1)^n}{(2n+1)^s} \]

Like the Dirichlet eta series, the alternating sum converges conditionally throughout the right half-plane \( \operatorname{Re}(s) > 0 \).

Function · 02.3

The Gamma function

The complex Gamma function \( \Gamma(z) \) is evaluated using the Lanczos approximation with parameters \( g = 7 \) and \( N = 9 \):

\[ \Gamma(z) \approx \sqrt{2\pi}\,(z+g-0.5)^{z-0.5} e^{-(z+g-0.5)} A_g(z-1) \]

where \( A_g(z) \) is the partial fraction expansion:

\[ A_g(z) = p_0 + \sum_{n=1}^8 \frac{p_n}{z+n} \]

The Chebyshev-derived Lanczos weights \( p_n \) used in both shader and C++ backends are:

Numerical Singularities & Overflow

Regions where function evaluation produces NaN or infinite values are rendered as dark terrain covered with glowing green grid squares reminiscent of The Matrix.

These regions typically arise near overflow conditions or severe floating-point instability in the GPU shaders. Rather than hiding such failures, the visualization exposes them directly as part of the numerical structure of the computation.

Function · 02.4

The Mandelbrot function

The explorer visualizes the Mandelbrot set by iterating the quadratic recurrence \( z_{n+1} = z_n^2 + c \) starting at \( z_0 = 0 \), where \( c \) is the coordinate in the complex plane. The iterations parameter controls the recursion depth.

Using the Zoom Factor

The Zoom Factor parameter controls the scale of the visible region in the complex plane. Larger zoom values progressively magnify smaller portions of the domain, rescaling coordinates before evaluating the function while preserving the same rendering pipeline. In the Mandelbrot visualization, deep zooms expose self-similar formations, filamentary boundaries, and intricate recursive structures characteristic of fractal geometry.

Section · 03

Riemann Sphere Projection

As an alternative to the flat complex plane, the explorer supports walking directly on the Riemann Sphere \(\widehat{\mathbb{C}} = \mathbb{C} \cup \{\infty\}\). Enable it under the Visualization tab in the menu, where you can also toggle radial Sphere height displacement and adjust the base Sphere Radius (which scales proportionally with Zoom Factor).

Geometry · 03.1

Floating-origin chart & stereographic projection

To reuse the flat chunk streaming and LOD system without floating-point drift, the player always stands at the apex \((0, 0, 0)\) of a sphere of effective radius \( R = R_{\text{base}} \cdot \text{zoom} \), while walking rotates the sphere underneath via an orthonormal orientation matrix \( \mathbf{B} \in SO(3) \).

Each vertex at flat chart offset \( \mathbf{c} = (x, z) \) around the player is first mapped to a local unit direction \( \mathbf{l} \in S^2 \) by an azimuthal-equidistant projection (preserving true arc-length distances near the player):

\[ d = \min\!\left(\frac{\|\mathbf{c}\|}{R},\,\pi\right), \qquad \mathbf{l}(\mathbf{c}) = \left(\frac{x}{\|\mathbf{c}\|}\sin d,\;\cos d,\;\frac{z}{\|\mathbf{c}\|}\sin d\right) \]

Rotating into the inertial sphere frame gives \( \mathbf{u} = \mathbf{B}\mathbf{l} \), where the north pole \((0, 1, 0)\) represents \( z = 0 \) and the south pole \((0, -1, 0)\) represents \( z = \infty \). Stereographic projection from the south pole maps \( \mathbf{u} = (u_x, u_y, u_z) \) to the complex coordinate \( z = \operatorname{Re}(z) + i\operatorname{Im}(z) \):

\[ z(\mathbf{u}) = \frac{u_x - i\,u_z}{1 + u_y}, \qquad \mathbf{u}(z) = \frac{1}{1 + |z|^2}\bigl(2\operatorname{Re}(z),\; 1 - |z|^2,\; -2\operatorname{Im}(z)\bigr) \]

When Sphere height is enabled, the raw function height \( h(z) \) is smoothly compressed via \( h_{\text{comp}}(z) = h_{\max}\tanh\!\bigl(h(z) / h_{\max}\bigr) \) with \( h_{\max} = 0.45\,R \) (preserving 1:1 heights for small values while keeping poles and deep valleys finite and continuous), and displaced radially outward along \( \mathbf{l} \):

\[ \mathbf{p}(\mathbf{c}) = \bigl(R + h_{\text{comp}}\cdot w_{\text{fade}}(\lambda)\bigr)\,\mathbf{l}(\mathbf{c}) - (0, R, 0) \]
Rendering · 03.2

Stereographic magnification & adaptive detail

Because stereographic projection compresses the entire exterior of the complex plane onto a small cap around the south pole \( z = \infty \), the local complex-units-per-world-unit magnification grows quadratically with \( |z| \):

\[ \lambda(z) = \frac{|dz|}{ds} = \frac{1 + |z|^2}{2R} = \frac{1}{R(1 + u_y)} \]

The renderer uses \( \lambda \) across several stages to keep both visual detail and frame rate comparable to plane mode:

  • Per-pixel resolution near \(\infty\): Chunks whose vertex spacing in complex units exceeds the resolution threshold automatically switch to per-pixel evaluation in the fragment shader, eliminating blocky polygon artifacts near \( z = \infty \).
  • Band-limited radial height: Where \( \lambda \in [2, 6] \), radial height displacement smoothly fades to zero (with exact product-rule surface normals) so under-sampled oscillations do not create jagged silhouettes.
  • Conformal minimap & Flow: The minimap expands its view radius proportionally to \( (1 + |z|^2)/2 \) and transforms the camera heading through the stereographic differential so it stays aligned after curved walking. The Flow overlay uses inertial cube-sphere UVs and the true tangent downhill gradient \( -\nabla h \), working both with and without radial height.
  • Domain & Target Riemann Sphere HUD widgets: Available in both plane and sphere modes under the UI tab, display interactive, draggable 3D Riemann spheres showing your current domain position \( z \) and live image \( w = f(z) \) (with \( 0 \) at the south pole, \( \infty \) at the north pole, and unit modulus on the equator) together with their recent trajectory trails.
Mode Behavior

Switching between the complex plane and the Riemann Sphere preserves your current complex coordinate \( z \). While in Riemann Sphere mode, multivalued branch portals and automated critical-line / Newton walks are disabled.
